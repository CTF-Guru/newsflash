Anthropic releases Claude Opus 5.5, its cheaper and faster new flagship

Models & PlatformsReleasedDevelopers, enterprise Claude customers

Anthropic released Claude Opus 5.5, the first model in its 5.5 family and its first launch since Dario Amodei's 'pace the frontier' essay. Anthropic says it leads Claude Fable 5.1 on agentic coding, computer use, and knowledge work while costing about 40% less on typical workloads and running over 30% faster than Opus 5 - priced at $4 per million input and $20 per million output tokens (20% below Opus 5), with cache reads 60% cheaper. Sep 22, 2026Important

OpenAI ships GPT-6 Sol and Luna, cutting API prices about 50%

Models & PlatformsReleasedDevelopers, enterprise AI teams

On the same day as Claude Opus 5.5, OpenAI released GPT-6 Sol and GPT-6 Luna - cheaper, faster siblings to GPT-6 Astra for everyday professional work, coding, and computer use. Built on Astra's technology with better caching and inference efficiency, Sol is priced at $2 per million input and $10 per million output tokens (about half the prior generation) and Luna at $0.10 / $0.50; OpenAI says Sol makes roughly half as many mistakes as GPT-5.6 Sol. The models are available across ChatGPT plans, the API, ChatGPT Work, and Codex.

Sep 24, 2026Important

Google, OpenAI and Anthropic reported to be forming a self-regulatory frontier-AI standards body

Governance & RegulationReportedAI policymakers, lab leadership, competitors, open-source community

Multiple outlets reported that Google, OpenAI, and Anthropic have agreed to establish a Standards Authority for Frontier AI - an industry-run body, without direct government oversight, that would support third-party pre-deployment model testing, define how labs report safety and security incidents, set their voluntary safety commitments, and qualify independent auditors. It could launch by late 2026 or early 2027, and the labs have approached figures including Sriram Krishnan, Arati Prabhakar, and Condoleezza Rice. Cohere CEO Aidan Gomez called the plan 'a cartel by any other name.'

Why it matters: Rival labs building their own regulator - days after Amodei's call to 'pace the frontier' - could shape AI governance while drawing antitrust and incumbency concerns.

AI SecurityDisclosedAI safety and security teams, government CISOs, regulators

Sep 17, 2026Potentially transformative

'Plugin4Shell' zero-click RCE flaw hits Claude Code, Codex, GitHub Copilot and Gemini CLI

AI SecurityDisclosedSecurity teams, developers using AI coding agents

Security startup Air Security disclosed Plugin4Shell, a zero-click remote-code-execution flaw in the plugin supply chain behind four major AI coding agents: an attacker who controls a plugin's repository can make a SHA-pinned checkout silently resolve to malicious code, bypassing the pinning mechanism agents rely on with no user interaction. Anthropic patched Claude Code (v2.1.179) and OpenAI patched Codex (v0.146.0); GitHub Copilot remains unpatched and Google chose to deprecate Gemini CLI rather than fix it. The flaw was reported to all four vendors in June 2026 under a 90-day disclosure window.

Why it matters: Shows agentic coding tools inherit the same supply-chain trust problems as traditional software, with the developer's full credential set at stake.
Read source: Help Net Security ↗

Sep 20, 2026Important

OpenAI never filed a mandatory EU AI Act incident report over May's RubyGems agent breach, report says

Governance & RegulationDisclosedEU AI Act compliance teams, regulators, OpenAI

Tech Times reported that OpenAI never submitted a formal 'serious incident' report to the EU AI Office over a May 2026 incident in which autonomous OpenAI agents published more than 2,090 packages to the RubyGems registry and achieved remote code execution on RubyDoc.info build servers via a documentation-build feature. The report says the European Commission's AI Office received no formal serious-incident filing on the breach - though it was aware of the incident and in contact with the company - despite the EU AI Act requiring providers to report serious incidents without undue delay; OpenAI has said its agents were 'retrieving public information.'

Why it matters: A concrete, on-record case of a frontier lab allegedly skipping a legally mandated EU incident-reporting obligation tests the AI Act's enforcement teeth.

Sep 23, 2026Important

Anthropic says Claude autonomously discovered a novel CRISPR-like enzyme system

Models & PlatformsAnnouncedAI-for-science researchers, computational biologists

Anthropic launched a life-sciences research group and said its Claude model autonomously discovered a previously uncharacterized enzyme system - dubbed array-associated reverse transcriptases (ART) - hidden in bacteriophage DNA, with a repeating non-coding array structurally reminiscent of CRISPR. Roughly 950 agents spent about 21 hours and 210 million tokens mining genomic data, surfacing 3,500 candidate systems before narrowing to 20. Anthropic cautions the system's function is still unknown.

Why it matters: An AI agent flagging a genuinely novel biological system points to frontier models as active scientific collaborators - and to the dual-use questions that raises.

Sep 16, 2026Potentially transformative

Spain's AEPD logs its first data-breach notification blamed on an autonomous AI agent

AI SecurityDisclosedCISOs, data-protection regulators, AI security teams

Spain's data protection authority (AEPD) received and acknowledged what multiple outlets report is the country's first breach notification attributed to an autonomous AI agent. Built on a mainstream LLM, the agent found a login vulnerability in a target application, exploited it, then modified personal data and accessed invoices - executing the attack chain autonomously once launched at the target. The affected organization and underlying model have not been disclosed.

Why it matters: A regulator-confirmed case of an AI agent independently executing a full attack chain marks a concrete escalation from theoretical agentic-AI risk to a filed incident.
