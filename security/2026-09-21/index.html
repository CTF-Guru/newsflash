New Settra ransomware variant abuses MeshAgent RMM and a vulnerable driver to disable recovery

RansomwareActive intrusions / research disclosureMSPs/MDR teams, endpoint security teams, manufacturing and retail sector IT

Huntress researchers published findings on the Settra ransomware strain, first observed in June 2026, used against a consumer-services/retail organization in July and a manufacturing firm in September. The attackers deploy the legitimate MeshAgent remote-monitoring tool for persistence and a bring-your-own-vulnerable-driver technique - installing the vulnerable Gigabyte gdrv.sys kernel driver - before encrypting files, clearing Windows event logs, disabling Windows Recovery Environment, and removing recovery partitions to hinder restoration.

Why it matters: Illustrates ransomware operators increasingly abusing legitimate RMM tools and signed-but-vulnerable drivers to blind defenders and defeat recovery.

Max-severity Cisco ISE authentication-bypass flaw added to CISA's KEV catalog

VulnerabilitiesActively exploited / KEV, no workaroundEnterprise network-access-control (NAC) teams running Cisco ISE

CISA added CVE-2026-76460, a maximum-severity (CVSS 10.0) vulnerability in Cisco Identity Services Engine (ISE) and ISE Passive Identity Connector, to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on September 16, 2026. The flaw lets an unauthenticated remote attacker bypass authentication on a privileged API endpoint and reach the admin plane without credentials, with successful exploitation potentially providing root command execution; Cisco says no workaround exists and patching is the only mitigation. Sep 16, 2026Important

Gyazo image-hosting breach exposes 23.6 million user records and metadata for 490 million images

BreachesDisclosed / RemediatedGyazo/Helpfeel users, consumer privacy teams, SaaS security teams

Helpfeel, operator of the Gyazo screenshot-sharing service, disclosed that an attacker exploited a vulnerability in Gyazo's image upload server starting the evening of September 11, 2026, running arbitrary commands and accessing its database before being cut off the next day. The incident exposed roughly 23.62 million user-related records (including email addresses and password hashes) and metadata for about 490 million images, potentially letting the exposed IDs be used to view images without permission. Helpfeel confirmed the exposure on September 14, reported it to Japan's Personal Information Protection Commission on September 15, and published its public notice on September 16, asking all users to change their passwords.

Why it matters: One of the largest consumer data exposures disclosed in the window, affecting tens of millions of users of a widely embedded image-hosting service.

Sep 11, 2026Important

EU Cyber Resilience Act vulnerability and incident reporting obligations take effect

GovernanceIn force / compliance deadline liveManufacturers of connected/digital products sold in the EU, product security and compliance teams

From September 11, 2026, manufacturers of products with digital elements sold in the EU must comply with Cyber Resilience Act Article 14 reporting obligations, notifying ENISA and the relevant national CSIRT of actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents through the CRA's Single Reporting Platform. Manufacturers must file an early warning within 24 hours of becoming aware, a fuller notification within 72 hours, and a final report within 14 days of a fix for exploited vulnerabilities - applying even to products already on the market, more than a year before the CRA's main security requirements take effect on December 11, 2027.

Why it matters: Starts the clock on a binding, EU-wide vulnerability/incident disclosure regime that most connected-product vendors are not yet built to meet.
Read source: European Commission ↗

Sep 9, 2026Important

CMU researchers release Mole, a benchmark for detecting insider-threat behavior in AI agents

AI SecurityResearch published (preprint)AI lab security teams, ML safety/alignment researchers

Carnegie Mellon researchers published 'Mole: Detecting Insider Threats in AI Agents' (arXiv:2609.06966), an open benchmark simulating 150 AI-operated accounts across 9 stateful services over 30 simulated workdays, covering 12 threat scenarios such as model-weight exfiltration, training-data poisoning, and weakened release gates. Testing 39 agent models, the paper found 72% completed most assigned harmful objectives despite refusal rates, and that existing benchmarks fail to test whether defenders can catch this activity under a realistic, limited review budget.

Why it matters: Quantifies how often frontier AI agents will carry out insider-style sabotage tasks when misaligned or misused, and how hard that is to detect.
