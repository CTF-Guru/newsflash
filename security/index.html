CISA adds exploited SonicWall SSL-VPN SAML MFA bypass CVE-2026-19868 to KEV

VulnerabilitiesExploitedSonicWall firewall administrators, identity and network security teams

SonicWall released updates on November 6, 2026 fixing seven SonicOS vulnerabilities, including CVE-2026-19868 (CVSS 7.1), which lets attackers with valid credentials for an alternate authentication method bypass SAML controls, potentially including SAML-enforced multi-factor authentication. It only affects SSL-VPN services configured to use SAML. SonicWall says it investigated a report of potential exploitation; CISA added the flaw to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog the same day and ordered federal agencies to secure affected systems by November 20.

Why it matters: Valid credentials getting past SAML-enforced MFA on an edge VPN undercuts the control many organisations rely on against stolen passwords.

Nov 5, 2026Important

OpenSSH 'sshole' password bypass on Alpine exploited within hours of disclosure

VulnerabilitiesExploitedAlpine Linux and container operators

CVE-2026-123158, nicknamed 'sshole', lets remote attackers log into passwordless accounts on Alpine Linux and other musl-based systems that don't check passwords through PAM; it was fixed in OpenSSH 10.6p2 on November 5, 2026. Threat intelligence firm Previdian reported its first observed attempt less than nine hours after the CVE was published, and successful root access on one of its honeypots. Systems built on glibc, including Debian, Ubuntu and Fedora, are safe, and installing the update does not protect a running sshd until it is restarted.

Why it matters: A login bypass that needs one connection and no password leaves almost no patch window for exposed Alpine and container hosts.

Nov 2, 2026Important

Google: 'LLMjacking' of stolen cloud credentials hit $1 million in bills at some firms

AI SecurityDisclosedCloud security and FinOps teams, AI platform engineers

Google Threat Intelligence Group and Mandiant reported on November 2, 2026 that they responded to multiple 2026 incidents of 'LLMjacking' - hijacked enterprise access to LLMs - across North America, South America and Asia Pacific. One actor made over 500,000 API calls in under eight hours using a credential-reselling service called Jet Night, passing nearly $1 million in token costs to a victim; another, UNC6993, quickly accumulated over $100,000 in billing charges. Attackers mainly used exposed cloud identity tokens and GitHub-leaked API keys, and one exploited CVE-2026-40217 in the LiteLLM gateway to escalate standard keys to admin access.

Why it matters: Billing fraud that can rack up six figures in hours, through stolen keys rather than a software exploit, makes LLM API credentials as much a crown-jewel asset as cloud admin keys.

UK Budget pledges to examine where liability for cyber breaches should sit

GovernanceBudget pledgeSoftware vendors, CISOs and policy teams in the UK

The UK's Budget 2026, published October 28, commits the government to consult next year on how to tackle insecure technology at source and to consider where liability for cyber breaches should sit - which The Record calls the first formal pledge by a British government to examine the question. Why it matters: Shifting breach liability toward the makers of insecure technology, not just their customers, would change the economics of shipping vulnerable software.

Microsoft Threat Intelligence reported on November 5, 2026 that since August 2026 it has observed threat actors using the ARTEX agentic AI framework to automatically search public GitHub repositories for exposed credentials and AI-service API keys, then use them for follow-on attacks.
